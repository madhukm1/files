💡 Reset makes your machine **deterministic**.

## 🌱 First: how digital hardware REALLY works (from ground up)

Forget VHDL for a moment.

### Real hardware has only TWO kinds of things:

1. **Wires + gates** → combinational logic
2. **Flip-flops** → memory

That’s it. Nothing else exists.

---

## 🧠 Flip-flop truth (this is the heart)

A flip-flop:

* **looks at inputs only at clock edge**
* **changes output only at clock edge**
* ignores everything in between

Think of a flip-flop like a **camera** 📸
It takes a photo **only when you press the shutter (clock edge)**.

No shutter → no new photo.

---

## ⏰ What “clock edge” REALLY means

If clock is:

```
0 ────┐    ┌────
      └────┘
```

There are only **two special instants**:

* **rising edge** (0 → 1)
* **falling edge** (1 → 0)

💡 **Only at those instants** flip-flops act.

Everything else is noise to them.

## ⚠️ Why simulation works but hardware fails (VERY IMPORTANT)

This happens when you **think like software**, not hardware.

### In simulation:

* signals change instantly
* no delays (unless you model them)
* no noise
* no metastability

### In real hardware:

* signals take time to travel
* clock arrives at different FFs at different times
* glitches exist
* reset might be slow or async

So:

> **Simulation is polite**
> **Hardware is brutal**

## 🧨 Common mistake example

### You write (wrong thinking):

```vhdl
if rising_edge(clk) then
  a <= b;
  c <= a;
end if;
```

### You *think*:

> “First a gets b, then c gets new a”

### Reality in hardware:

* BOTH flip-flops sample **old values**
* updates happen **together**

So:

```text
c gets OLD a, not new a
```

This is why **non-blocking (`<=`) exists**.

## 🧱 Blocking vs Non-blocking (simple analogy)

### Blocking (`=` in Verilog, variables in VHDL)

Think **calculator**:

* line by line
* immediate effect

### Non-blocking (`<=`)

Think **camera shutter**:

* everyone captures at same time
* update happens together later

👉 Flip-flops MUST behave like cameras
👉 So flip-flops MUST use non-blocking behavior

---

## 🧠 Why hardware needs non-blocking

Because real flip-flops:

* all sample inputs **at the same instant**
* all update outputs **after that instant**

Non-blocking models this exactly.

Blocking does NOT.
